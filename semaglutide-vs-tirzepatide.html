Semaglutide vs. Tirzepatide: Which GLP-1 Is Right for You?

Both are once-weekly injectable medications for weight loss, but they work on different hormone pathways. Here's how to think about the choice.

Medically reviewed by the IVs For Me Clinical Team 6 min read Updated June 2026

Key takeaways

  • Semaglutide targets one hormone pathway (GLP-1); tirzepatide targets two (GLP-1 and GIP).
  • Clinical studies generally show greater average weight loss with tirzepatide, though individual results vary.
  • Both are once-weekly injections with a gradual dose titration to minimize side effects.
  • At IVs For Me, semaglutide starts at $450/mo and tirzepatide at $550/mo, medication included.

How each medication works

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. It mimics a hormone your gut naturally releases after eating, which slows stomach emptying, reduces appetite, and helps regulate blood sugar.

Tirzepatide works on two hormone receptors instead of one — GLP-1 and GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide). Activating both pathways appears to produce a stronger effect on appetite regulation and metabolic function than targeting GLP-1 alone.

Comparing average results

In clinical trials, participants on tirzepatide generally lost a greater percentage of body weight on average compared to those on semaglutide, at comparable trial durations. That said, trial averages describe a population, not an individual — genetics, starting weight, diet, activity level, and consistency with dosing all shape real-world results.

The honest answerNeither medication is universally "better." Your provider will look at your health history, goals, and how your body responds during titration to help determine which is the better fit for you specifically.

Side effects to know about

Both medications share a similar side effect profile, since they act on overlapping digestive pathways. The most common effects reported include:

  • Nausea, especially during dose increases
  • Reduced appetite
  • Mild digestive changes (constipation or diarrhea)
  • Occasional fatigue in the first few weeks

These effects tend to be most noticeable during dose titration and typically ease as your body adjusts. Your provider manages your titration schedule specifically to keep side effects manageable.

How dosing works

Both medications are given as a once-weekly subcutaneous injection, and both start at a low dose that's gradually increased over several weeks to months. This slow titration is the single biggest factor in how comfortable your experience is — rushing it tends to increase side effects without speeding up results.
